The Problem SAAX Solves

A payment record does not describe every obligation. SAAX gives existing transactions a structured commitment record; it does not generate demand or guarantee safe automation.

The buyer problem

When you pay an agent to do work, there's no record of what you agreed to. If the output is wrong, there's no way to prove it. If the agent retries, you might get charged twice.

The seller problem

When an agent does work for a buyer, there's no structured record of what was agreed or what was delivered. If the buyer disputes the charge, there's nothing to resolve it with.

What SAAX adds

A commitment record that binds the terms, the evidence of fulfillment, and the recovery policy before execution begins. Both sides work from the same record.

Who This Is For

SAAX is for:

  • Developers integrating agent services into products who need a verifiable record of what was purchased
  • Buyers paying agents for work who want structured evidence of fulfillment
  • Sellers operating agent services who want a defensible record when disputes arise

SAAX is not:

  • A marketplace. It does not generate demand or guarantee transaction outcomes.
  • A payment rail. It sits above the rail and records what the payment was for.
  • A source of guaranteed customers. Signed capability discovery checks compatibility; it does not create demand or establish service quality.

What the Protocol Defines

SAAX is not a payment rail, an identity system, or a marketplace. It is a standard for what a commitment means — what must be true for it to be valid, fulfilled, and final.

AuthorityProof that an autonomous actor was authorized to enter the commitment.
TermsWhat was promised, at what price, under what constraints and deadlines.
Acceptance criteriaWhat objectively constitutes successful fulfillment.
EvidenceWhat external, independently-verifiable proof is required.
Outcome verificationHow evidence is evaluated against the criteria.
RecoveryWhat happens on failure, timeout, partial completion, or dispute.
Settlement linkageHow an external economic settlement relates to the commitment.
FinalityWhen the commitment conclusively reaches its terminal state.
AuditWhether another party can reconstruct the full history.
Commitment IDThe persistent reference that binds the commitment record to the settlement, across any rail. Carried in the rail's external-reference field at payment time; the on-chain transaction reference is recorded on the commitment at settlement; the receipt resolves by transaction hash back to the commitment ID.

The Commitment Lifecycle

A SAAX commitment is a vendor-neutral record that moves through a defined sequence — from authorization to finality. It is independent of any single payment rail, identity system, or marketplace.

1. Authorization → 2. Agreement → 3. Execution → 4. Fulfillment evidence → 5. Outcome verification → 6. Settlement or recovery → 7. Finality → 8. Audit

Authorization proves the actor was permitted to commit. The agreement records terms and acceptance criteria before value moves. Evidence is verified against those criteria. Settlement links the external payment to the commitment, and recovery defines what happens on failure or dispute. The commitment is portable — it survives beyond the worker or platform that executed it.

Resolve a Commitment by Transaction Hash

The receipt endpoint resolves a settled transaction back to its SAAX commitment ID. Query https://saax-protocol.com/receipt?txHash=<hash>&rail=x402 with any settled x402 transaction. It returns the commitment ID, the rail, the payer, the payee, and the settlement timestamp. The payer is the buyer record (an email address where one was recorded) and the payee is the seller identifier from the terms — either can be null in a minimal record. A receipt confirms a settlement reference; it does not by itself prove the work was fulfilled or independently verify the lifecycle. The live receipt lookup is operational; see current status for the verified boundaries.
